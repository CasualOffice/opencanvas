OpenCanvas by CasualOffice · Compare

A self-hosted, open-source alternative to Miro

Miro is a hosted online whiteboard. In OpenCanvas the whiteboard is one block of a collaborative document, next to the notes, databases and spreadsheets it belongs with, and the whole thing runs on your servers inside your own application.

Try the live demo Quick start Documentation

The short answer

For large workshops on an endless board, with a big template library and facilitation tools, Miro is the stronger whiteboard. If you want diagrams and sticky notes to live inside the document where the decisions are written down, and you need to self-host or embed that in your product, OpenCanvas is built for it.

Where OpenCanvas fits better

  • The board is part of the document. One session, one save, one file: the whiteboard, the meeting notes and the task database sit together, with named cursors and Follow.
  • The everyday whiteboard tools. A context menu, tool lock, a style panel with rotation, smart guides, quick-connect, frames you can present, starter templates, and copy or export as PNG or SVG.
  • Diagrams you can bring with you. Shape libraries, connectors that stay attached to shapes, connector routing and layout, and import from draw.io and Excalidraw; export as SVG or PNG.
  • Self-hosted and embeddable. It runs on your servers, your application signs who may open which board and with which role, and the file is saved back to your storage.
  • Apache-2.0. No per-seat licence; build it into your own product.

Where Miro is stronger

  • Whiteboarding depth. Miro is a dedicated whiteboard product with facilitation features such as timers and voting, and a large library of templates. OpenCanvas presents frames and has a few starter templates; its board is simpler.
  • Scale of one board. Miro is designed for very large boards with many people. An OpenCanvas board is a block of a document and is tuned for the size of a working session.
  • Integrations and apps. Miro connects to many work tools and has desktop and mobile apps. OpenCanvas runs in the browser and integrates with Jitsi Meet, Nextcloud, Mattermost channels and Element / Matrix.
  • Maturity. OpenCanvas is an early release with best-effort support and no production SLA.

Questions people ask.

More in the documentation FAQ.

Is the OpenCanvas whiteboard an infinite canvas like Miro?

It is a whiteboard block inside a document, with zoom and full screen. It suits diagrams, sketches and sticky-note sessions that belong to a document; it is not built to replace a dedicated board for very large workshops.

Can I bring diagrams from other tools?

Yes: OpenCanvas imports draw.io and Excalidraw files when the host turns import on, and exports boards as SVG and PNG. There is no Miro import.

Can I embed the whiteboard in my own product?

Yes. That is the main use: your backend signs a short-lived launch token naming the user, the file and the role, and the editor opens in an iframe or the open-canvas web component. Your app keeps the file.

Is it free?

Yes. OpenCanvas is open source under the Apache-2.0 licence, free to self-host, modify and embed, including in commercial products. Support is best effort, with no production SLA.
